Tonight at the Tower

A card game on a paper stage, played with one thumb.

Coming soon to Google Play · for Android 11 and newer

The game

A deck of cards in a toy theatre

A deckbuilding roguelike, staged with woodcut paper puppets behind a curtain and a row of footlights. Each run starts with a small deck. You add cards and relics as you climb the Tower, and when a run ends you start a new one from the first floor.

Played with one thumb

Portrait only, with everything you press placed where a thumb reaches. Drag a card to play it. A setting moves the controls to suit your left hand or your right.

Three acts, with a boss at the end of each

Thirty floors of fights, shops, rests and events, laid out on a playbill that is drawn afresh for every run. There is an interval between the acts.

One lead: the Necromancer

He raises minions that serve for a few turns and then leave the stage, and he wears his enemies down with Curse. He is the only character in the game.

Seven Encores

Win a run and the next Encore opens: the same climb with one more hardship added to all the ones before it. There are seven.

Stop whenever you like

The game saves as you play, in the middle of a fight too, so you can put the phone down and take the run up again later. It needs no connection to play.

What it costs

Free, with ads you can remove

The game is free. Your first five runs have no ads. After that an ad may show at a break in play: when a boss is beaten, and when a run ends. One purchase through Google Play, made once, removes the ads for good.

Privacy

No account, and the detail is your choice

There is no sign-in, and the game never asks for your name, email or contacts. Your progress is kept on your phone, and your phone's own backup can carry it to a new one. Run data, which is the cards you pick and how your fights and runs go, is sent only if you say yes when the game asks after your first run. A small daily count with no identifier in it stays off on a phone whose region is set to the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland unless you switch it on, and is on elsewhere unless you switch it off. Settings has a switch for each. The ads come from Google, whose ad software uses your phone's advertising ID, and in those same countries Google's form asks what you allow before the first ad.
