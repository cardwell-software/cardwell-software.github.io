Unpack — Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 12, 2026

Unpack is built to work entirely on your device.

What we collect: nothing.

Unpack has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no advertising SDKs.

Unpack cannot reach the internet at all

This is stronger than a promise not to. Unpack does not declare Android's INTERNET permission, and an Android app without that permission cannot open a network connection of any kind — not to us, not to anyone. You can verify this yourself on your own device: open Android Settings, then Apps, then Unpack, then Permissions.

The app's build enforces it too. Every build checks the app's complete, final permission list against a fixed set, and fails if anything outside that set appears — including any permission arriving through a third-party library.

Files you open

Unpack reads and writes files on your device, which is its entire purpose. Those files are never uploaded, copied off the device, or indexed anywhere, because the app has no way to send anything anywhere.

All files access

On Android 11 and later, Unpack may ask for the "All files access" permission. It is never requested on first run, and the app works without it — extracting to your Downloads folder needs no permission at all. It is used for two things: extracting to a folder you choose anywhere on your storage, and measuring what is using space across the whole device. Without it, Android hides other apps' files from Unpack, so a storage measurement would be silently incomplete; Unpack refuses to show one rather than showing a wrong one. You can revoke the permission at any time in system settings.

Opening a file in another app

Unpack shows images and plain text itself. For anything else, tapping a file hands it to an app you already have — a video player, a PDF reader, and so on. Once a file is handed to another app, what that app does with it is governed by that app, not by Unpack. This is the one thing the rest of this page does not cover, and we would rather say so plainly.

What is stored on your device

Unpack saves three small preferences, and nothing else:

  • which destination you last extracted to, as a category such as "Downloads" — never a path
  • how you like file lists sorted
  • the date of your last storage scan

No file names, no folder paths, and no passwords are ever written to disk. Passwords you type for encrypted archives are held in memory only and are gone when the app closes.

Backups

Android may back up app data to your Google account. Unpack limits this to the three preferences above. Copies of archives you open are explicitly excluded from both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.

Data sharing

We do not share data with anyone, because we do not collect any.

Children

Unpack does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
